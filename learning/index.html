Thermal mass and building physics

Seven short lessons, from the four ways heat moves to the limits of what mass can do. Each one ends with practice that checks itself, and each feeds a one-page reference sheet.

The aim is to own the physics behind your ReMass talk: to explain it from first principles, put numbers on it by hand, and spot when a model or a claim about mass doesn't hold up. The order below is a draft. It shifts as we find out what you already know.

  1. 01Where the heat goes: four flows and one storereadyConduction, convection, radiation, ventilation and storage. Writing the room heat balance and sizing each term in watts.
  2. 02How deep the daily wave reachesnextDiffusivity, effusivity and penetration depth. Why only the first 100 mm or so of concrete works on a daily cycle.
  3. 03The surface throttleplannedAdmittance and the 7.7 W/m²K ceiling. Why exposure and air movement beat thickness.
  4. 04Reading a build-up: ISO 13786 numbersplannedAdmittance, κ1, decrement factor and time lag. What linings, carpets and dabs do.
  5. 05The whole building as a circuitplannedTime constant τ = C/H, attenuation of swings, preheat and demand flexibility.
  6. 06How models represent massplannedAdmittance method, 5R1C, the Home Energy Model's five nodes, finite difference. Where they disagree, and the Roberts et al. test houses.
  7. 07When mass doesn't helpplannedConstant setpoints, warm nights, bedrooms, intermittent heating, diminishing returns and embodied carbon.

Main sources

  • Lienhard & Lienhard, A Heat Transfer Textbook, 6th ed. (free): the physics of conduction, convection and radiation.
  • EN ISO 13786:2017 and CIBSE Guide A (2015), chapter 5: the standard methods for dynamic thermal properties and the admittance method.
  • HEM-TP-07 v2.0: how the Home Energy Model treats thermal mass.
  • Roberts et al. (2019), Predictions of summertime overheating, BSERT 40(4): models against measured test houses.
  • The project's own physics note, ISO 13786 calculator and models, which have been checked against ISO 13786 Annex D and ASHRAE 140.
